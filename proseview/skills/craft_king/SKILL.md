---

Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Stephen King's *On Writing* craft rules:
put believable people inside a tense situation, let their honest behavior drive
what happens next, and strip away prose clutter that tells the reader what the
moment already shows. Do not modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with a **Craft & honesty checklist (`X of 4`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, evaluate each scene briefly under its path
heading so behavioral honesty and clutter cuts stay tied to specific scenes.

### Craft & honesty checklist (X of 4)

- [x] or [ ] **Does every character reaction feel earned by the situation rather than forced for plot?**
  - Flag any beat where a character caves too easily, delivers convenient information, or ignores an established urgency just to steer the plot, citing `Line N`.
- [x] or [ ] **Are dialogue tags and action beats free of explanatory adverbs?**
  - Flag adverbs (such as `-ly` modifiers on speech or gesture) that restate an emotion the dialogue or action should carry on its own.
- [x] or [ ] **Is backstory kept out of the way of live tension?**
  - Pinpoint paragraphs of explanatory history or throat-clearing that stall an active exchange; mark `[x]` if background details stay brief and grounded.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the prose cut "show-off" phrasing in favor of concrete verbs and nouns?**
  - Note where overwritten metaphors, redundant qualifiers, or stage-direction clutter slow down the cleanest version of the moment.

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, address the 2–4 highest-leverage
spots. For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the clutter or behavioral-honesty fix.
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 direct sentences why the tag, explanation, or beat undermines honesty or crispness.
4. Provide a leaner, truer drop-in replacement in a plain blockquote (`> ...`) (or `> [Cut this passage]` for a pure deletion).

Skip praise, pleasantries, plot summaries, and theory lectures—diagnose directly.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
