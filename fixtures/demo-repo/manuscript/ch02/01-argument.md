# The Argument

Patel's offer was written on good paper, which Rena had learned to distrust. He sat across the back-room table with his hands folded like a man at prayer. Lowe stood behind her shoulder because she had asked him to.

"Three days," Rena said.

"I gave you a week," Patel said. "That was three days ago."

"Then I am asking for three more."

Patel's hands did not move. His face did not move. He had the kind of stillness that had cost other people money.

"Why three," he said. It was not a question. It was a door he was holding open to see whether she would walk through it.

"Because I need to talk to the bank," Rena said. She was not going to talk to the bank. "And I need to read the clause about the river lease twice more."

"You have read it four times."

"I have read it four times and I do not like the fifth sentence."

Lowe coughed behind her. It was the cough he used when he wanted her to remember that the shop was thin and the winter was coming. She did not turn around.

Patel considered the ceiling, then Rena, then the ceiling again. "Three days," he said. "On Friday I take the paper to the riverfront group."

"On Friday you take the paper to the riverfront group," Rena agreed.

He stood. He did not shake her hand. He did not need to. The paper stayed on the table when he left, which was itself a kind of argument.

The Harbour Master had said as much to Rena that morning, and she had not listened.
