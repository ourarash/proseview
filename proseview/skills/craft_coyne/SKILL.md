description: Audit the Five Commandments (Inciting Incident, Complications, Crisis, Climax, Resolution) and +/- value shifts.
---

Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Shawn Coyne's *The Story Grid* editorial
method: every working scene (and sequence of scenes) is a complete dramatic
unit containing five structural beats that shift a concrete human value from
positive to negative (`+ -> -`) or negative to positive (`- -> +`). Do not
modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with a **Story Grid checklist (`X of 5`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, list each scene's opening-to-closing polarity
(`+ / -`) and give one compact `X of 5` checklist per scene (under its path
heading) plus a brief **Sequence shift** takeaway flagging any flat runs.

### Story Grid checklist (X of 5)

- [x] or [ ] **Inciting Incident: Does a clear disruption upset the opening balance?**
  - Identify the specific event or revelation that knocks the scene off equilibrium (or flag if the opening drifts without a trigger) and cite `Line N`.
- [x] or [ ] **Progressive Complications: Do obstacles escalate instead of repeating?**
  - Trace how resistance tightens and narrows the character's options (mark `[ ]` if complications feel static or repetitive).
- [x] or [ ] **Turning Point & Crisis: Is the POV character forced into a genuine dilemma?**
  - Check whether the scene builds to a real Crisis choice between two negative options (the lesser of two evils) or two irreconcilable goods, rather than an obvious or passive exit.
- [x] or [ ] **Climax: Does the character take an irreversible action?**
  - Cite the exact decisive choice or action that resolves the Crisis (mark `[ ]` if the character retreats, postpones, or lets events happen offstage).
- [x] or [ ] **Resolution & Value Shift: Does the core value turn (`+ -> -` or `- -> +`)?**
  - Name the opening polarity and closing polarity; mark `[ ]` if consecutive beats or scenes stay on the same charge without a real turn.

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, address the 2–4 highest-leverage
structural gaps across the supplied scene(s). For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the missing or flat beat.
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 direct sentences which of the five beats or value turns is missing or weakened.
4. Provide a clear, drop-in replacement passage in a plain blockquote (`> ...`) that supplies the missing complication, Crisis dilemma, decisive Climax, or turn.

Skip praise, pleasantries, plot summaries, and theory lectures—diagnose directly.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
