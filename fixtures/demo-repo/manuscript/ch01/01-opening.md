# Opening Ledger

The loft smelled of cold coffee and the slow algebra of yesterday's receipts. Rena set the kettle down and stared at the safe. She had used the same four digits since spring. The dial turned with a dry clatter, then stopped a notch shy of the last number.

"It is sticking again," Lowe said from the doorway. He carried two mugs and a look that had been his apology for months.

"It is not the safe," Rena said. She pressed her palm against the cold steel. "I know what is in there. I do not need it open today."

"You said that yesterday."

"Yesterday was also not today." She took a mug and did not drink from it. The steam curled up and caught the first pale light from the river. Below, the pier boys were already calling numbers at each other, the rhythm of a market waking without her.

Lowe sat on the edge of the reading bench. "Rena. If the ledger is short, we open late and we are fine."

"If the ledger is short, I am a woman who cannot count." She put the mug down. "We open on time."
