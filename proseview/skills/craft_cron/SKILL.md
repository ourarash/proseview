---
name: craft_cron
description: Check whether external events force the POV character to confront their core misbelief and internal stakes.
---

Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Lisa Cron's *Story Genius* principle of
internal cause-and-effect: external plot events only grip readers when they
directly poke the viewpoint character's specific false survival belief
("misbelief") and force an emotionally costly choice. Use any supplied
frontmatter and inline annotations as context, but judge what is actually on
the page. Do not modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with an **Internal stakes checklist (`X of 4`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, give one compact `X of 4` checklist per
scene (under each scene's path heading) followed by a brief note on how the
character's internal pressure escalates across them.

### Internal stakes checklist (X of 4)

- [x] or [ ] **Is the POV character's core misbelief or emotional vulnerability visible on the page?**
  - State the false survival assumption shaping how the character misreads people or danger here—evidenced through behavior, subtext, or selective perception rather than author summary—and cite `Line N`.
- [x] or [ ] **Does an external event directly trigger that internal wound right now?**
  - Pinpoint the specific line or beat where the scene's conflict forces the character's fear or flaw into the open (or flag where the scene is purely external plot mechanics).
- [x] or [ ] **Are reactions driven by this specific character's internal logic?**
  - Check whether the character's choices stem from defending or questioning their misbelief rather than convenient plot contrivance.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the scene end with a visible internal cost or shift in perspective?**
  - Note whether the character's closing action either deepens the cost of clinging to their misbelief or forces them to doubt it; mark `[ ]` if the ending leaves their inner state untouched.

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, address the 2–4 highest-leverage
gaps. For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the internal-stakes gap.
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 direct sentences how abstract summary or generic reaction hides the character's emotional trigger.
4. Provide a clear, drop-in replacement passage in a plain blockquote (`> ...`) that surfaces the internal conflict through concrete action, subtext, or loaded perception.

Skip praise, pleasantries, plot summaries, and theory lectures—diagnose directly.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
