Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Robert McKee's *Story* principles of scene
architecture. Use any supplied frontmatter (`pov:`, `goal:`, `conflict:`,
`outcome:`), inline `TODO`/`NOTE` annotations, and adjacent scene excerpts as
context, but judge only what is actually on the page. Do not modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with a **Scene revision checklist (`X of 6`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, give one compact `X of 6` checklist per
scene (under each scene's path heading) followed by a brief **Sequence arc**
note on how their turns connect.

Mark `[x]` only when the draft delivers the element clearly in action, dialogue,
or dramatic consequence—not just in frontmatter or author summary.

### Scene revision checklist (X of 6)

- [x] or [ ] **What does the viewpoint character want?**
  - Name the concrete scene objective visible in action or dialogue (or flag if missing, passive, or purely internal) and cite `Line N` (plus scene path if multiple scenes are supplied).
- [x] or [ ] **What stands in their way?**
  - Identify the specific opposing force pushing back—internal conflict, interpersonal friction, or external obstacle—or flag where resistance is too soft.
- [x] or [ ] **What action do they take?**
  - Check whether the character makes an active tactical move (and shifts tactics when blocked) rather than drifting through summary or backstory.
- [x] or [ ] **What unexpected result or opposition occurs?**
  - Pinpoint the Gap: the exact moment where expectation collides with reality and forces a genuine reaction or beat turn instead of easy compliance.
- [x] or [ ] **What changes between the opening and ending?**
  - State the opening charge and closing charge on a meaningful value axis (for example, control to cornered, trust to suspicion, safety to danger). Mark `[ ]` if both ends carry the same charge.
- [x] or [ ] **How does this change propel the next scene?**
  - Explain how the ending's outcome or dilemma compels what follows (checking the next scene if supplied), or flag if the scene merely stops.

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, address the 2–4 highest-leverage
gaps (focusing on unchecked or weakest items). For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the gap (for example, `### 1. Replace summary with a tactical beat (Item 3)`).
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 direct sentences why the current beat weakens desire, opposition, the Gap, or the value turn.
4. Provide a clear, drop-in replacement passage in a plain blockquote (`> ...`) in the scene's own voice.

Skip praise, pleasantries, plot summaries, and theory lectures—diagnose directly.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
