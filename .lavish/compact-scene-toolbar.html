Focused UI review · scene header

Make the toolbar disappear from the writing—not from the writer.

Replace the current wrap-prone control shelf with one 48px desktop toolbar, progressive disclosure, and a three-mode visibility preference. Auto-hide should be the default; pinned and hidden remain explicit writer choices.

Recommendation: compact + adaptive, not merely smaller

The main defect is hierarchy. Fourteen controls compete at once, presentation settings sit beside core actions, and the flex layout wraps exactly when the Discuss dock reduces width. A smaller font would preserve the underlying problem. The right fix is to keep location and frequent actions visible, move appearance and developer actions into two menus, and make visibility behavior a preference.

Scene 3 · Café

The coffee had gone cold before Mira admitted she had been watching the door. Outside, rain combed the avenue into silver lines.

She turned the cup by its handle. “I got here early enough to regret it.”

The chair opposite stayed empty. Her notes waited beneath one palm, the revised address written once in ink and twice in pencil.

At the end of the bar, someone laughed too loudly. Mira looked up, then away, and the toolbar slipped out of the reading space with the motion.

When she reversed direction, it returned—not as a shelf of settings, but as a quiet way home.

She read the first paragraph again. The work was still the work; the interface had finally stopped insisting otherwise.

The three modes

One component, one preference, three predictable behaviors. No separate focus-mode implementation is needed.

Recommended default

Auto-hide

Pinned at the top until the writer scrolls down; reveals on upward scroll, keyboard focus, top-edge hover, or a shortcut.

  • Best reading-space/action-access balance
  • Freeze while a menu is open
  • Disable animation with reduced motion
Predictable

Always shown

A 48px sticky bar remains visible. Ideal for editing, keyboard-heavy navigation, and writers who dislike moving chrome.

  • Stable target positions
  • No motion or rediscovery cost
  • Still far smaller than today
Maximum focus

Hidden

The bar retracts to a quiet top-edge reveal strip. The same shortcut and top-edge interaction restore it temporarily.

  • Maximum manuscript space
  • Must remain an explicit opt-in
  • Never hide while focus is inside

Implementation shape

This is deliberately a small redesign of one owner—not a new component framework.

01

Restructure

Replace the current flat control list with leading location, compact primary actions, Appearance, and More.

02

Responsive priority

Keep one row. Move actions into overflow by priority instead of wrapping. Never shrink the title to zero.

03

Visibility state

Persist auto | pinned | hidden. Use scroll direction, focus-within, menu-open, and reduced-motion guards.

04

Prove it live

Test docks, long paths, light/dark, 1024px, 200% zoom, keyboard focus, reload persistence, and scroll recovery.

Important: the existing Focus button currently hides only the extra controls while leaving the 59px bar. It should become a shortcut into the same toolbar visibility model, or be retired after migration; maintaining two competing “focus” systems would create state drift.

Why this is the “right way”

The proposal follows current platform and accessibility guidance rather than copying a fashionable screenshot.

Prioritize, then overflow

Apple’s toolbar guidance says to choose items deliberately and move lower-priority actions into overflow as width narrows. That maps directly to the Discuss-dock failure.

Dense desktop bars are legitimate

Material documents dense desktop top bars and scroll behavior that hides on downward movement and reveals on reverse movement.

Sticky cannot obscure focus

WCAG 2.2 requires keyboard focus not be entirely hidden by sticky content. Use scroll padding/margins and force reveal on focus.

Zoom changes the strategy

W3C recommends making sticky regions static or user-toggleable in constrained reflow. At high zoom, default to pinned/static and consolidate aggressively.
