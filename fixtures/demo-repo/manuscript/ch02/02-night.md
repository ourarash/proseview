# Night Ledger

The loft was quiet in the way that only small rooms are quiet at one in the morning. Rena had opened the ledger for the third time. The numbers had not changed. The bank statement on the left did not agree with the column of sums on the right. Two hundred and eleven dollars had walked out somewhere between Wednesday and Friday, and none of the slips she had pinned to the board could tell her where.

She set the pencil down. She picked it up. She set it down again. Outside the river made its low, indifferent sound, and the gulls that hunted at night made their thin, interested one.

She thought about Patel's hands, folded the way her father used to fold his before a hard conversation. She thought about the river lease clause and the three sentences that did not quite mean what the other sentences meant. She thought about Lowe's cough.

At some point the thinking stopped being thinking and became a shape behind her eyes. She put her head on the open ledger and slept there, and dreamed of numbers that refused to add up even when she begged them politely.
