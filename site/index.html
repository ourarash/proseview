Your book stays a plain folder on disk.

Your manuscript folder holds one folder per chapter and one Markdown file per scene. Proseview reads that structure directly—no database, no import, and no lock-in.

  1. BookThe manuscript folder: word count, goal, and pace.
  2. ChaptersOne folder per chapter, in the order you name them.
  3. ScenesOne Markdown file per scene, with optional metadata (POV, setting, timeline) at the top.
my-novel/
└── manuscript/
    ├── ch01/
    │   ├── 01-the-letter.md
    │   └── 02-the-station.md
    └── ch02/
        └── 01-aftermath.md

Write

The page you read is the page you write on.

  • Press E and the typeset page becomes the editor. Cmd/Ctrl+S saves and returns you to reading.
  • Story bibles, plans, and any other Markdown or YAML file are editable in place too.
  • Search the whole book with Cmd/Ctrl+K, and choose from six themes and seven fonts.

Revise

Instant prose checks, with no AI required.

  • 13 instant, local style checks (no AI): repetition, filter verbs, passive voice, crutch words, POV slips, and more.
  • Plain-English manuscript takeaways on pacing and vocabulary variety, plus a chapter-by-chapter character chart.
  • Track today's words, pace, and streak, and see exactly which words were added or cut in every scene.

Plan

See every storyline at once.

  • Tag scenes with a storyline (thread: in the scene header) to see every plot thread as its own lane across the book.
  • Compare chapter proportions and scene lengths side by side to spot chapters that run long.
  • Give a scene a label, such as Main Plot or Flashback, and its color shows in the sidebar, the scene list, and the timeline.

Optional AI

Your own Codex, Claude, or Antigravity—only when you ask.

  • Connects to the local Codex, Claude, or Antigravity (Gemini) CLI already signed in on your computer—no API keys, no Proseview subscription, and no limits from us.
  • Three agent tabs side by side, each keeping its own conversation as you move between files.
  • A real agent, not a single-shot chat box: it searches the whole book, reads your story notes, edits multiple files at once, and looks things up on the web.
  • Completely optional and never runs on its own. Every edit shows a before-and-after diff with one-click Undo all.
  • Choose what each conversation sends with your question (full scene, beat map, story notes), and see the tokens each reply used.

Beat Map

See how a scene moves.

  • Plots an estimated tension curve for the scene alongside its moments in order: dialogue, action, internal thought, and silent reaction.
  • Click any moment to jump straight to that paragraph.
  • Generated by Codex, Claude, or Antigravity when you ask—without touching your prose—saved with the book, and flagged out of date whenever the scene changes.

Scene reviews

Review notes you can preview and apply.

  • Run a quick critique, deepen and layer (subtext and sensory detail), comedy review, or craft review through McKee's Story, The Story Grid, Story Genius, Save the Cat!, On Writing, or Bird by Bird.
  • Every note quotes the passage, explains what isn't working for the reader, and offers a replacement in the scene's own voice.
  • Compare suggestions side by side with your prose, check the notes you want, add your own guidance, and apply them—every change shows a before-and-after diff with one-click Undo all.
  • Review one scene, its neighbors, or a whole chapter, and choose what to focus on and how many notes to get.

The Group

Let your agents debate.

  • Put two or three AI agents in one conversation—using Codex, Claude, or Antigravity—each focused on a different angle: story, voice, and (optionally) continuity.
  • They read the same scene and respond to each other—agreeing, pushing back, or passing. Different models catch different issues.
  • Ask them all, or @mention one. A recap summarizes what they agreed on, the decisions left to you, and each suggested edit as Before and After.
  • Send any one-on-one agent chat to the Group for a second opinion: the other agents weigh in, the first agent responds, and you get a combined recap.

Illustrations

A picture for every scene.

  • One click asks Codex, Antigravity, or Claude to illustrate the scene in one of six styles, from woodcut to oil painting.
  • The image waits above the scene until you insert it, and you can ask for revisions right in the chat that made it.
  • Inserted images save beside the chapter as ordinary image files, so Obsidian, VS Code, and GitHub display them too.

Publish

Export ready-to-publish books.

  • Export an EPUB for every e-reader, and a print-ready paperback PDF from 5 × 8 to 6 × 9 inches or A5.
  • Export a Word document for your editor's tracked changes, or standard manuscript format for literary agents.
  • Includes plain-language preflight checks for EPUB and print formatting—no Pandoc, LaTeX, or Calibre to install.

Remote mode

Read, edit, and dictate from your phone.

  • Run proseview --remote to open your book from your phone over your private Tailscale network—no extra accounts, passwords, or pairing.
  • Read on, tap a paragraph to add a TODO or note, make quick edits by thumb or voice dictation, or ask Codex, Claude, or Antigravity about the scene.
  • Drafts stay safe on your phone until you save, with conflict protection so phone and computer edits never silently overwrite each other.

Fits the way you already write.

Obsidian

Point Proseview at your vault and nothing moves. Wikilinks, callouts, and embeds stay exactly as you wrote them.

Git, for backup

Your book is plain text, so Git keeps every draft. Push it to a private GitHub repository and it is backed up off your computer. Each commit shows exactly what you changed, and Proseview reads your commits and saved scenes for your goals and writing streak.

VS Code

Open any scene in VS Code, Cursor, or Zed with one click and keep it open beside Proseview. A change saved in either one shows up in the other.

Install Proseview

On your computer

macOS, Windows, and Linux · nothing to install first

Open Terminal (on a Mac, press Cmd-Space and type Terminal), paste this line and press Return:

curl -LsSf https://ourarash.github.io/proseview/install.sh | sh

Open PowerShell (press the Windows key and type PowerShell), paste this line and press Enter:

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ourarash/proseview/main/site/install.ps1 | iex
  • It asks where your book is: press Enter to start a new one, or drag in your book's folder.
  • It adds a Proseview launcher (on your Desktop on macOS and Windows, or in your apps menu on Linux). Open it anytime to launch your book; your files stay on your computer.
  • Run the same line again to update.

Already use Python? pipx install proseview (or pipx install "proseview[claude]" for the Claude tab), then run proseview in your book's folder.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Proseview is open source under the MIT license, with no paid version, no subscription, and no account. If you use the optional AI panel, Codex, Claude, or Antigravity bill you under your own plan with them.

Where is my book stored?

On your computer, as plain Markdown files in your own folder. If you start a book in the browser, it is kept in that browser's storage on your device, and clearing the site's data deletes it, so use Download it to keep a copy. Proseview has no server that receives your book. To back it up, keep the folder in Git and push it to a private repository, on GitHub or anywhere else.

Do I need Obsidian?

No. Any folder of Markdown files works, and Proseview can create the files for you. If you do use Obsidian, Proseview works on your vault without changing how it is written.

Do I need Git?

No. Proseview works on any folder. Git is the easiest way to back the book up and keep every draft, and in a Git folder Proseview also shows your goals: today's words, your pace, the writing streak, and what changed.

Do I need AI?

No. Everything except the agent panel works without it, and the agent panel does nothing until you ask it something.

Can I try it without installing?

Yes. The sample book shows the dashboard for Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, and you can start a book in your browser with nothing to install. A book started there stays in that browser until you download it.

Can I move a book from the browser to my computer?

Yes. Download it gives you the book as a zip of its folder. Unzip it and run proseview open path/to/folder.
