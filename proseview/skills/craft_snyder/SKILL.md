Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Blake Snyder's *Save the Cat!* craft tests
for reader momentum, hidden exposition, and emotional turning points. Do not
assume any prior familiarity with the book—apply each criterion below directly
to what is written on the page. Do not modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with a **Scene momentum checklist (`X of 4`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, give one compact `X of 4` checklist per
scene (under each scene's path heading) and note whether emotional polarity
alternates across the sequence.

### Scene momentum checklist (X of 4)

- [x] or [ ] **Is necessary exposition buried inside active physical business or conflict?**
  - Flag any passage where backstory, lore, or explanation stops the scene ("talking heads" or static summary), or confirm where information rides naturally inside an immediate goal or clash, citing `Line N`.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the scene's emotional polarity genuinely shift (`+ -> -` or `- -> +`)?**
  - Name the opening emotional charge and closing emotional charge; mark `[ ]` if the scene repeats the same emotional note from start to finish (or across consecutive scenes).
- [x] or [ ] **Does the protagonist earn reader investment through active, readable choices?**
  - Check whether the POV character makes clear, concrete moves under pressure (rather than passively enduring or complaining) so the reader stays aligned with their stakes.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the ending plant a forward hook into what comes next?**
  - Pinpoint the closing beat's unanswered question, threat, or shift in plan (mark `[ ]` if the ending merely winds down or resets to neutral).

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, address the 2–4 highest-leverage
gaps. For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the momentum or exposition fix.
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 direct sentences how static exposition, flat emotional charge, or a soft exit slows momentum.
4. Provide a clear, drop-in replacement passage in a plain blockquote (`> ...`) that hides exposition inside action, sharpens conflict, or turns the ending into a hook.

Skip praise, pleasantries, plot summaries, and theory lectures—diagnose directly.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
