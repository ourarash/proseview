---
name: scene_continuity
description: Check this scene’s dates, facts, and character knowledge against supplied story evidence.
---

Check the active document against the supplied manuscript and reference
material. Keep findings centered on this document; do not turn the pass into
an audit of unrelated problems elsewhere. Respect any specific continuity
question or focus supplied by the writer.

Check established dates and ages, locations and travel, possessions and physical
states, relationships, event order, and character knowledge. Compare facts at
the same story time. File order need not be chronological, and a character's
claim, memory, guess, or lie is not automatically an objective fact.

Show the evidence on both sides of a possible conflict. Missing explanation
alone is not a contradiction. A later revelation does not by itself prove that
a character could not know something earlier. Treat disagreements with outlines,
frontmatter, or planning notes as questions for the writer unless the writer
has identified those notes as authoritative.

Anchor each finding to the affected passage in the active document. Explain
the incompatible fact or dependency and identify the other supplied source.
If the evidence leaves more than one valid interpretation, state the decision
needed rather than inventing story history or declaring one version correct.
Offer a minimal replacement only when the intended fact is established.
No supported continuity findings is a valid result.
