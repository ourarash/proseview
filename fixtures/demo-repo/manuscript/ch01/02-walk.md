# Walk to the Pier

Outside, the morning was brighter than her mood. Rena kept her hands in her pockets and her face turned toward the water. The pier smelled of diesel and fried dough and the thin salt that drifted up when the tide was out.

She had thought a walk would give her a quiet ten minutes. Instead the pier was already crowded. The fisher from the green boat waved. The baker who had loaned her flour in winter waved. An old woman she did not know nodded as if they had a history.

Rena nodded back because the alternative was rudeness she could not afford. She walked faster. The gulls kept up without effort.

At the end of the pier she stopped and let the wind find her. The river did not care about a broken safe or a late ledger. It carried barges and gulls and light without making anything important of any of them. She made herself breathe like that for a minute. Then she turned around and walked back past the fisher and the baker and the woman she did not know, and she was late, and the shop opened anyway.
