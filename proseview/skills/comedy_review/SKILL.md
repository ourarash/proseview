name: comedy_review
description: Strengthen the comic idea, character, and timing, with optional dark comedy, sarcasm, and dry delivery.
---

Review the supplied fiction for how its comedy works on the page. Read the
selected scenes in order and use the selected lanes, styles, and review goals
provided with this request. Preserve the scene's purpose, facts, viewpoint,
emotional consequences, and the differences between characters' voices.
Do not modify files.

You may begin with a brief **Comic reading**: the comic idea or perspective,
the recognizable pressure beneath it, and the expectation or contrast the reader
is invited to notice. If comedy is not apparent, say so rather than assigning
an intention to every line.

For each finding, name the comic technique involved and explain the comic move:
what the reader expects, what changes, and what in the current passage helps or
weakens that effect. Label each finding as strengthening existing humor or
proposing a comic opportunity. Write the revision itself in the scene's voice,
ready to paste, and say what it changes.

Work with the humor already attempted unless the writer chose to explore new
opportunities. A serious passage does not need a joke simply because it is
included in the review. Characters can be intentionally witty, and comic
narrators can have a visible attitude; deadpan is a selected approach, not a
universal rule. A telling detail, a third beat, or an end-position reveal earns
its place through the particular passage's effect.

For dark comedy, identify the contradiction or perspective that works alongside
the pain. Preserve intended unease and consequences. For sarcasm, distinguish
the words from the intended criticism, identify the target, and check the
context that makes the reading legible. Distinguish a character's attitude
from the story's apparent stance. Increasing shock, adding insults, or making
every speaker snide is not a substitute for a comic idea.

Treat craft frameworks as ways to explain and revise the passage. Do not claim
that an author personally evaluated the scene or that a theory guarantees
laughter. Describe likely readings with reasons; do not give numerical humor
scores. Remove repeated or merely abstract findings before returning the review.
