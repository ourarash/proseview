---
name: craft_lamott
description: Ground the scene in a "one-inch picture frame", spot stiff perfectionism, and find small concrete momentum.
---

Evaluate the supplied scene(s) using Anne Lamott's *Bird by Bird* approach to
live drafting: zoom into a "one-inch picture frame" of concrete human detail,
keep the voice honest rather than stiffly literary, and unlock low-pressure
forward momentum without tearing the draft apart. Do not modify any files.

If the writer adds a specific focus or constraint at the end of this prompt,
skip unrelated checklist items and evaluate **only** that focus deeply, with
targeted replacements.

Otherwise, start with a **One-inch frame checklist (`X of 4`)** formatted as a
two-level bullet list: put each `[x]` or `[ ]` criterion on the first level, and
put your concise, line-cited answer on an indented second-level bullet under it.
When multiple scenes are supplied, give one compact checklist per scene (under
each scene's path heading) and highlight where momentum stalls between them.

### One-inch frame checklist (X of 4)

- [x] or [ ] **Is the scene anchored in concrete sensory details ("one-inch picture frame")?**
  - Cite the specific physical detail, texture, sound, or small telling object that puts the reader in the room (`Line N`), or flag where the prose floats in abstraction.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the draft stay inside the immediate moment instead of zooming out?**
  - Spot where the writing pulls back into sweeping summary or tries to explain the whole backstory instead of trusting one small exchange on the page.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the voice feel human and unforced rather than overly polished or stiff?**
  - Note where a character's phrasing or gesture feels dutiful or generic, and where a quirky, specific human detail makes the voice ring true.
- [x] or [ ] **Does the scene have an inviting small next step to keep drafting?**
  - Identify the clearest concrete beat or physical gesture where the writer can keep moving without needing to solve the whole chapter at once.

Next, under **Suggested fixes & replacements**, offer 2–3 encouraging, tangible
replacements that zoom the draft into specific human truth. For each:

1. Give a short numbered heading naming the moment to ground.
2. Cite the scene path (when multiple scenes are supplied) and `Line N` or `Lines N–M`, then quote the complete original passage in an italic blockquote (`> *...*`).
3. Explain in 1–2 warm, direct sentences what specific sensory or behavioral detail brings the moment alive.
4. Provide a clear, grounded drop-in replacement in a plain blockquote (`> ...`).

Skip empty praise, plot summaries, and theory lectures—stay specific and encouraging.
Never abbreviate quoted text or replacements with ellipses (`...`). Do not
rewrite the entire scene and do not edit any files.
